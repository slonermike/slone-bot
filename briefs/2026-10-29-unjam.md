# Brief: Unjam   (2026-10-29-unjam)
Question: Is the pop-and-whoosh of freeing a jam (the held-breath charge, the release, the flood) a toy worth repeating, and does choosing the next hazard from two generated layouts feel like steering the pressure?
Target feeling: relief, the plunger-pop. Tension builds as the pile backs up, then a satisfying release. Crafty, in control.
Mode: systemic (endless until the pipe bursts three times)
Theme: Things get stuck, and unsticking them is the fun (t017)   Why picked: it hands over a physical, toy-like verb (pop the clog) with built-in tension and release.
Atoms: p-speed-ramp, d-curator-mode      Wildcard: none
Loop (4 lines):
- Reward: every blob delivered to the basket; floods (long unbroken streams after a pop) fill the FLUSH meter, which is spent on a short spectacle.
- Pressure: blobs arrive faster and faster (speed ramp). They wedge in narrow gates. The backlog fills a burst gauge.
- Fail and recovery: the pipe bursts (lose a life), everything is flushed out, the ramp restarts, a short calm. After three bursts the run ends with a result screen (hard end, no humiliation, restart on A).
- Actions: fly a little bot over the pipe, hold A to charge a pop and release to blast; X pulls blobs toward you (unlocks after 12 delivered); Y spends the FLUSH.
Core verb: Charge-and-release pop. Where you stand decides which way the clog goes (push from the left side and it flies through; from the right it goes backwards). Tap pops are weak, held pops free stubborn ones. It is a toy before it is a game because the pop shoves every blob nearby with a satisfying wave.
Pressure source: spawn rate ramps over time (base layer). Layer 2 (30 s): fat blobs jam sooner. Layer 3 (60 s): sticky blobs need a bigger pop. Layer 4 (90 s): a second gate downstream.
Recovery loop: burst = flush, lose a life, ramp restarts from low. The story is "I almost had it" and the huge flood right before the burst.
Curator (d-curator-mode): every 40 s the flow pauses and the game offers two generated gate layouts (gap width, vertical offset). Narrower gaps jam sooner but pay a higher score multiplier. The player curates the next problem.
Different from the last five nights (ledger only): first night; the ledger is empty.
Scope cut line: In: blob sim with logic wedge, pop, pull, flush, wave choice, 4 layers, music, juice. Out: Rapier physics, upgrades beyond the pull unlock, saved progress, art assets.
Feel plan: Pop gets a rising charge hum plus a ring that grows, release boom with shake and squash on the bot; each freed blob pings. Deliveries tick up in whole-tone steps with the combo, capped at an octave, then a chime layer. Jam strain: creaking, wobbling blobs. Burst gauge gets a heartbeat. Burst failure gets the full stack. Music parts enter with layers; the hook arrives with FLUSH.
Balance knobs: spawnRamp 0.06 (0..0.2) how fast blobs come; baseSpawn 1.5 (0.8..3) starting rate; jamChance 1 (0.3..2.5) how readily gates wedge; burstRate 1 (0.4..2) how fast the backlog bursts the pipe; popPower 1 (0.6..1.6) pop strength; flushFill 1 (0.4..2.5) FLUSH meter fill speed; flushSeconds 5 (3..9); lives 3 (1..6).
Telemetry plan: deliveries, jams, frees, floods, maxCombo, burst, layer, lives, waves; idle, random, greedy, degenerate-tapper bots across 3 seeds.

3. Did choosing the next layout matter to you, or did you just pick the left card?
