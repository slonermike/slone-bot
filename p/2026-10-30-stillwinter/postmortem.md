# Post mortem: Stillwinter
Question and target feeling: Does tower defense feel fresh when the world is frozen and the only way to move time is to break your own defenses? Target: tense, crafty stewardship, a held breath then a thrilling release.
What shipped: a 90-second-of-thawed-time tower defense on an isometric ice table. Main verb: shatter (X) a spire you placed (A) to release time; nothing moves while frozen. Pressure: snow-star enemies advance only during released time, layered by dawn (Drifts, Rams, Gliders, Wisps, the Thaw) plus warm fronts; forward pressure: 4 spire unlocks, fault lines (double time), Cold Snap (Y, a power cascade). Genre came from a seeded roll over 5 candidates (tower defense, contraption builder, colony sim, stacker, clockwork factory): `runs/2026-10-30-stillwinter/genre-roll.mjs`.
Core before polish: yes. The bare sim (no render) was played by bots first; the economy needed three fixes (surprises.md) before it was a game. Judged by bot outcome spread and decision counts, not by feel.
Did it work (honest read): feel checklist 44 pass, 2 fail (no trails; idle bot proves nothing moves, which is the theme itself, not back pressure), 9 untested (all the [Slone] feel items), 4 n/a. The shaky items: reward-cue meaning, whole-tone ladders, whether Cold Snap is big enough.
Measured (telemetry, 3 seeds, sim only): greedy completes all 3 (92 to 95 s of thawed time), 28 to 31 kills, loses 3 to 4 hearts; random bot completes 0 of 3 in 120 s (dawn 10 to 62%); shatter-spam probe fails or stalls (51 avg score vs greedy 165). longest frozen stretch (greedy) 0.5 s, 5 enemy kinds, 2 warm fronts per run. Real-time in headless Chromium: 60 fps, inputToAudioMs 1.7; inputToVisualMs reads 137 ms because the software renderer is slow, so it says nothing about the Deck.
Believed (labeled, I cannot play or hear it): shattering should feel good because every break is stacked (hit-stop, shake, glass plus crunch plus a whoosh that climbs a whole tone per chain link, orange ripple, floating +time). The first 30 seconds before Crystal unlocks may feel stingy (a spike costs net 2 ice per 3 s of time). Frozen planning may feel calm or may feel like stalling; unknown.
Degenerate strategies found: place-and-shatter on one cell near the beacon (fixed by making plain spire blasts weak and Crystals the bombs). Remaining: crystal "mines" next to the path are strong; a second bot (chain-cluster) plays for chains: it loses fewer hearts than greedy (0 to 3 vs 2 to 4), reaches max chain 3 to 5 and uses Cold Snap 1 to 2 times, so chains are the stronger line but not a runaway.
What I cut or ran out of time for: more maps (3 only, chosen by seed), spire upgrades, a "B" cursor jump. Added late: Polar Night (Y after dawn).
Surprises (follow the fun): the economy cliff, the spam probe beating the greedy bot, and one object with three reasons to break it; no pivots. See surprises.md.
Biggest risk for Slone to check: the tempo of time. "Time released per shatter" (3 s) against enemy speed decides whether this feels like held breaths or like fidgeting. Try that knob first, then Wave frequency and Starting ice.
Reusable tech merged to main: none (a test fix for the themes script's next-id check, which assumed the pool never shrinks).
Assets sourced: all synthesized or drawn in code (system font). Music composed by the scaffold generator (D dorian, one loop, five stingers).
Theme picked and why: t018 "Time only moves when something breaks": it makes the clock the resource and gives tower defense a new verb.
Themes added: "A failure that melts into the materials to rebuild"; "One object with three reasons to break it"; "Perfect information and no clock: only scarcity pushes back".
Next time (proposed lesson, one data point): when time is frozen between decisions, execution difficulty disappears, so pressure must come from scarcity and from flow-driven, telegraphed threats; and a spend-to-act economy needs failure to refund materials or it snowballs.
Run health: `npm install` had to be run first (node_modules absent, preflight crashed on the missing playwright package, then passed). The themes unit test asserted the pool never shrinks, so any night's resolve would have broken `npm test` on main; fixed in the test. Files opened outside the core set: docs/tech/07, 09, 10 and the music types (all allowed docs). No other nights' code opened. Wall clock under 1 hour of the 3.5-hour budget at first publish; the extra time went into polish and content (Polar Night endless mode after dawn, frost shells, trails, crack decals, a chain-playing bot).
