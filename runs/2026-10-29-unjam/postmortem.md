# Post mortem: Unjam
Question and target feeling: Is the pop-and-whoosh of freeing a jam a toy worth repeating, and does choosing the next hazard from two generated layouts feel like steering the pressure? Target feeling: relief, the plunger-pop after a held breath.
What shipped: fly a little plunger-bot over a pipe; hold A to charge a pop, release to free wedged blobs so the backed-up pile floods into the basket. Pressure: a speed-ramped blob stream that wedges in gates, filling a burst gauge; layers add fat blobs, sticky blobs and a second gate; every 40 s you pick one of two generated gate layouts (tighter pays more). Pull unlocks after 12 deliveries; combos fill a Flush (player-triggered spectacle). Hard end after three bursts (soft reprieve between: pipe clears, ramp restarts, a calm beat).
Core before polish: yes, the sim was proven with the bare bots before any render code existed. Judged by bots only: idle dies by about 105 s on every seed (first pressure about 21 s), greedy delivers 143 to 365 blobs vs idle's 17 to 25, hold-A-forever reaches about 23% of greedy's score. I cannot tell whether the bare core is interesting to a human.
Did it work (honest read): feel checklist (runs/2026-10-29-unjam/feel-checklist.md): yes 43, no 1 (entrance jitter), n/a 3, untested 12 (the [Slone] items, fps and latency, clipping). Shaky: the pop's feel, the stuck-blob readability, the wave choice.
Measured (telemetry, 3 seeds, 120 s sim): greedy score avg 5403 (seed 1: 374, seeds 2 and 3: 7223 and 8613; the gap is whether the Flush got triggered), delivered 143 to 365, jams 28 to 36, floods 10 to 33, deaths about 1.7; idle deaths 3, first pressure 20 to 24 s; random deaths 3. Real-time fps in this software-GL sandbox 5.4 (demo: 4.7, so parity; unmeasured on real hardware). inputToAudioMs not measured in the sandbox.
Believed (labeled, I cannot play or hear the build): the hold-and-release will feel good because charge ticks climb a step per tick; the first jam is a gift at 6 s so the verb is learned early; the clear window after a pop is what makes floods; the music's hook-on-Flush should feel earned. Unverified: whether tapping pops 'no budge' feels fair, whether sticky blobs (grip 1.8) are too hard.
Degenerate strategies found: standing left of the gate and holding A (auto-fires full pops every 0.9 s) scores about 23% of greedy and still floods sometimes. It works because full pops free everything in range. Mitigation in place: a big pop has a longer cooldown and pushes the pile back into the gate. Watch it.
What I cut or ran out of time for: Rapier physics (circles use my own separation), upgrade tree, per-gate distinct blob types, a pop that can be aimed (it is radial).
Surprises (follow the fun): (1) the first version re-wedged freed blobs the same step, which made the whole game whack-a-mole. A short "clear window" at the gate after an unjam turned it into floods, and that became the reward the combo and Flush hang from (reinforces the target feeling, kept). (2) Standing on the wrong side of a jam shoots it backwards (BACKFIRE), which I did not design as a feature but is a nice positional skill.
Biggest risk for Slone to check: pop charge time (0.55 s to full) and burst pace. If early game feels too fast, lower "How fast the backlog bursts the pipe"; if the pop feels weak, raise "Pop strength".
Reusable tech merged to main: smoke test now picks the demo card even if other prototypes are listed (smoke.mjs); themes unit test no longer hard-codes the pool size. Nothing in src/core.
Assets sourced: all synthesized and code-drawn. Music is composed with the scaffold's generator.
Theme picked and why: "Things get stuck, and unsticking them is the fun": it gives a physical toy verb with built-in tension and release.
Themes added: "The reward is whatever was waiting behind the obstacle"; "You choose which problem arrives next, and tighter problems pay more".
Next time (proposed lesson, one data point): after any obstruction-clearing verb, give the moment just after a generous window; the release is the reward.
